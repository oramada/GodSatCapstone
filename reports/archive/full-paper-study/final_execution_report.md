# GoDSAT study execution report

**Status:** the paper's design-of-experiments workflow and requested orbit/dropout extensions have been executed in a Python replacement system. A second-seed, 48-date full-catalog tasking run now independently confirms the simulated burst-loss result is consistent across two random streams. This is not a reproduction of the original GoDSAT implementation or its claimed improvements: the original MATLAB source, pretrained Q-tables, exact baseline, and calibrated sensor model were not available. The paper's numerical Results section is a placeholder, so its stated 11–14% custody gain and 9% slew-energy reduction are not reproduced or treated as targets.

The paper does not specify a sensor-dropout experiment. The dropout analyses below are a separate extension requested during this project.

### Empirical burst-persistence proxy from DUST

From 22 CASSIOPE/FAI sessions, the 4,284-frame annotated positive-track spans contain 47 unlabeled internal frames in 27 gap runs. The conditional missing-state persistence proxy is (47−27)/47 = 0.426, with a 22-session cluster-bootstrap 95% interval of 0.071–0.550. The calculation is independently reproduced from the checksum-verified raw DUST archive in `outputs/dust-burst-persistence-empirical/`. These are annotation gaps, not confirmed missed exposures; obscuration, annotation omission, and track behavior remain possible causes. The estimate supports a bounded burst-process sensitivity, not a hardware failure rate. The earlier unscaled 0.426-proxy policy run is superseded by the cadence-corrected study below.

The independently reconstructed 1 Hz image timestamp grid adds a distinct capture-record gap proxy: 94 nominal image slots are absent within 4,378 expected slots across annotated RSO transit windows (2.15% pooled; 2.60% equal-weight session mean, 95% session-cluster bootstrap interval 0.33–6.40%). The raw archive and timestamp audit reproduces this count and interval. It measures gaps in the released image record, not confirmed failed camera commands; scheduling, archive selection, or downlink can also explain absent frames. Among the 4,284 captured images in those windows, 47 have no manual RSO label, which combines detection failures and annotation omissions.

An additional held-out image-signal check now separates labeled detections from local background controls by observation session. A threshold set at the 95th percentile of controls from the other 21 sessions detected 3,712/4,237 labeled positions (87.61% pooled; 86.05% equal-session mean, 95% session-bootstrap interval 79.71–91.74%) and flagged 493/8,821 held-out controls (5.59% pooled). Of the 47 unlabeled internal-gap frames, 26 (55.3%) had local contrast above their held-out session threshold; this was 15/19 one-frame gaps and 10/12 two-frame gaps, but 0/13 frames in the single long 13-frame gap. This is a cross-validated brightness diagnostic, not a validated object detector or confirmed RSO truth. The long-gap interpolation is especially uncertain (p95 error 7.21 pixels in its separate cross-validation), and apparent signal may be another object, star, or artifact. The thresholds, per-gap classifications, uncertainty intervals, and 22-fold raw-source reproduction are in `internal_gap_image_signal_audit.json` and `outputs/full-system-audit.json`.

## Paper workflow

The four-regime, current-active-payload-host campaign executed the paper's 16-run, seven-factor resolution-IV screening design; its exact 15-run face-centered central composite design (CCD); a 31-trial Bayesian reference; and five-seed held-out retrains. The selected screening factors were actor width, learning rate, and entropy. The campaign and its run/checkpoint inventory are recorded in `outputs/all-orbits-paper-exact-active-hosts/` and independently reconciled by `campaign_integrity_audit.json`.

The campaign's regenerated [`report.md`](../all-orbits-paper-exact-active-hosts/report.md) contains the screening, half-normal, response-contour, residual, and held-out validation figures with the numeric tables.

The PDF's printed defining relation conflicts with its stated generators E=ABC, F=ABD, and G=ACD. The implementation follows those generators; their products give I=ABCE=ABDF=ACDG=AEFG=BCFG=BDEG=CDEF. As required for diagnosis, 12 additional independently seeded CCD center retrains were run for the four-regime active-host study. The resulting 27 observations provide 12 pure-error degrees of freedom and five lack-of-fit degrees of freedom. The quadratic surface is a saddle, and lack-of-fit rejects the objective model (p=0.024) and custody model (p=0.003). The optimizer's constrained point is therefore not accepted as a validated setting. The consolidated hash-checked evidence is in `outputs/full-system-audit.json` under `replicated_center_lack_of_fit`; the full replication report and independent validation are in `outputs/all-orbits-active-hosts-center-replication/`.

On the held-out 2026-10-01 scenarios, the DOE-selected setting did not establish an improvement over the configured replacement baseline. Averaged over five retrains and four orbit regimes, custody was 0.1569 ± 0.0172 for baseline, 0.1595 ± 0.0315 for DOE, and 0.1618 ± 0.0265 for Bayesian optimization (95% Student-t half-widths). The paired DOE-minus-baseline custody change was +0.0026 ± 0.0375; its interval includes zero. DOE's paired slew-proxy change was −0.00009 ± 0.00534, and false-track-proxy change was +0.00123 ± 0.01025; both intervals also include zero. Slew and false-track values are proxies in this simulator. GPU time was zero because this campaign ran on CPU; no calibrated electrical-energy measurements are available. The configured baseline is not the unavailable published GoDSAT baseline.

## Plain-language results and scope

The replacement system has completed the PDF's experimental workflow: it screened seven settings, fit and checked quadratic models, and compared candidate policies. Those runs do not reproduce the paper's reported improvement because the PDF does not provide numeric results and the original GoDSAT policy, training assets, and baseline are unavailable. The replacement study's own diagnostics also reject the quadratic response model for important outcomes, so an optimizer recommendation is not a validated best setting.

For the added dropout question, removing sensor opportunities in simulation lowers the number of satellites the scheduler keeps under observation. This effect appears in several sensitivity runs, but its size depends on the assumed outage process and the simulated sensor model. Ground archives provide observations and conditional missing-detection proxies; they do not include complete commanded-attempt and failure logs. Therefore this study cannot yet say how effective GoDSAT is against real ground-sensor dropout.

The historical orbit archive covers four years and is reconstructed at four-hour cadence for the full catalog, with independent source and propagation checks. A separate daily, one-hour, all-catalog paired tasking campaign is still running and is only checkpointed through its latest audited date. It is not continuous four-year operations. Catalog-wide attitude history is also unavailable; attitude coverage currently comes from limited mission data and cannot be assigned to every satellite.

### Durham optical gap-rate and persistence sensitivity

The Durham wide-field catalogue gap proxy parameterizes a paired synthetic burst-loss sensitivity using a 14.5527% conditional in-field catalogue-gap fraction and a 0.32458 missing-to-missing transition fraction. The observed frame cadence is about 8.835 seconds (median of ten nightly median cadences), while this simulator advances in 60-second steps. We therefore rescaled the two-state Markov persistence to the simulator cadence, preserving the stationary outage fraction; the effective persistence per 60-second step is about 0.14555. All 192 monthly date/regime scenarios completed, and the independent audit validated 384 cache hashes, five checkpoints, 1,920 policy rows, and 19,200 paired dropout rows. Simulated custody changed by −0.691/−0.682 pp (SGP4/AWP-J2) in LEO, −0.551/−0.565 pp in MEO, −0.214/−0.215 pp in GEO, and −0.467/−0.469 pp in HEO. The AWP-minus-SGP4 dropout-effect intervals include zero in all four regimes.

The conditional rate's 95% night-cluster interval (11.78–16.35%) was also rerun at both endpoints with the same cadence conversion. All 192 scenarios completed and passed the independent audit at each endpoint. The central and endpoint runs are in `outputs/awp-policy-monthly-durham-gap-time-scaled-2022-2026/` and the corresponding `...time-scaled-ci-low/high-2022-2026/` folders. The earlier unscaled results in `awp-policy-monthly-durham-gap-sensitivity-2022-2026/` and the unscaled `...durham-gap-ci-low/high...` folders are superseded because they applied an approximately 8.8-second persistence directly at a 60-second simulation step. Durham contains processed positive detections, not raw frame-health or failed-attempt logs: its rate conditions on already detected targets bracketed by positive observations, and its persistence describes catalogue omissions rather than hardware failures. These are corrected, cadence-aware sensitivities, not measured natural dropout effectiveness or a catalog-wide miss probability.

### AWP-J2 comparison against operational ILRS CPF positions

To check orbit-model behavior against an external operational product, AWP's two-body-plus-J2 model was propagated from historical GP/TLE epochs for four ILRS retroreflector targets, then compared with their CPF positions in ITRF. One observation was sampled per nonoverlapping 72-hour bin, yielding 1,536 samples from 1,528 historical element arcs. The independent row-level audit passed. Across every target, AWP-J2 residuals were materially larger than SGP4 residuals: AWP-J2 versus CPF median separations were 13.916 km (LAGEOS-1), 13.896 km (LAGEOS-2), 8.882 km (LARES), and 7.445 km (Starlette), compared with SGP4 medians of 0.179, 0.351, 0.490, and 0.332 km, respectively. Date-clustered 95% intervals for the AWP-minus-SGP4 separation difference were above zero for all four targets. This is evidence that the current AWP-J2 setup, which omits drag, maneuvers, covariance, and assimilation, does not match these sampled operational products as closely as SGP4; CPF is not independent orbit truth, and four targets do not validate the full catalog. Reproduce with `PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/validate_awp_against_ilrs.py` and audit with `PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/audit_awp_ilrs_cpf.py`. ## Full-catalog target-capacity reference

A separate 48-date extension replays all fresh, valid archived catalog targets, rather than the learned policy's fixed 18-target sample. Each one-hour episode uses six study-selected active-payload hosts, 60-second steps, historical SGP4 updates, and a greedy urgency/slew rule; paired schedules use zero and 20% burst loss. The 192 date/regime groups produced 384 treatment rows and passed a structural/statistical audit that verifies all 192 host-scenario hashes and independently recomputes the date-clustered intervals. The episode pool averaged 25,359 catalog IDs. The six hosts completed an average of 358 actions and 320 detections per hour. Mean all-target custody was 0.082% with no added loss and 0.066% with 20% burst loss; the four regime-specific 20%-minus-zero custody differences range from −0.0153 to −0.0175 percentage points, with clustered intervals excluding zero. These low all-catalog custody values expose the capacity gap between six hosts and the full target population. This is a greedy reference, not the trained DOE policy; hosts are current-active payloads backcast through history, outages are injected, and episodes sample one hour per month rather than continuous operations. Results and audits are in `outputs/full-catalog-tasking-monthly-2022-2026/`.

### Attitude solution source quality

At the simulator's 60-second cadence, the independently checked CASSIOPE records contain 1,497,458 star-tracker source samples, five coarse sun-sensor/magnetometer samples, and 504,137 samples with no valid quaternion. For valid quaternion samples, the nearer definitive attitude source is 2.77 seconds away at the median and 59.94 seconds away at the 95th percentile. The source handbook gives nominal uncertainty ranges for these source classes, but the archive does not provide per-sample attitude covariance or residual truth; the spacing is not an error estimate. These data can help define future attitude-quality scenarios, while actual pointing-error simulation still needs validated error bounds.
